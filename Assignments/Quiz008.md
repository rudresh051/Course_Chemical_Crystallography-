## Question 1

> Dynamic disorder occurs because of  

Thermal vibrations of atoms Different  
orientation of a molecule Different  
conformation of some part of a molecule  
Different location of an atom/ion/molecule in the asymmetric unit

✅ **Thermal vibrations of atoms**

### Why?

**Dynamic disorder** occurs when atoms or molecules are **moving/vibrating over time** rather than being fixed in one position.

In crystallography, atoms are always undergoing thermal motion. If this motion is significant, the electron density becomes spread out, which can appear as **dynamic disorder**.

The other options describe **static disorder**:

* **Different orientation of a molecule** → static orientational disorder
* **Different conformation of some part of a molecule** → static conformational disorder
* **Different location of an atom/ion/molecule in the asymmetric unit** → static positional disorder

### Easy way to remember

> **Dynamic = moving → thermal vibrations**
> **Static = multiple possible arrangements → orientation, conformation, position**

## Question 4

> Why do we need to do absorption correction?

Crystal may move during data collection  
If the crystal contains heavy elements  
Crystal may get a crack during the data collection  
Crystal may have another crystal sticking to it  

The correct answer is:

✅ **If the crystal contains heavy elements**

### Why?

**Absorption correction** is needed because the X-rays passing through a crystal can be **absorbed by the atoms in the crystal** before reaching the detector.

This effect is especially significant when the crystal contains **heavy elements**, because heavy atoms generally have stronger X-ray absorption.

For example, crystals containing elements such as **Pb, Hg, or U** can have substantial absorption.

### Why not the others?

* ❌ **Crystal may move during data collection** → affects reflection positions/intensities, but this is not absorption correction.
* ❌ **Crystal may get a crack** → damages the crystal and can affect data quality.
* ❌ **Crystal may have another crystal sticking to it** → this is a **twinning/multiple-crystal** or sample-quality issue, not absorption correction.

### Remember

> **Absorption correction = compensate for X-rays being absorbed by the crystal.**
> **Heavy elements → stronger absorption → correction becomes particularly important.**
